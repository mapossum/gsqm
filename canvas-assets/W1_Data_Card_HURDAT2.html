Data Card — NOAA HURDAT2 (Atlantic Hurricane Database)

Used as the Week 1 demo dataset in Lab 0. Model example of how every future Data Card in this course is structured.

What is it?

HURDAT2 (the "revised Atlantic Hurricane Database") is NOAA's authoritative record of every known North Atlantic tropical cyclone from 1851 to the present. It contains best-track information — position, maximum sustained wind, minimum central pressure, and storm status — at 6-hour intervals for every named storm plus many unnamed ones.

For Lab 0 we use only a small subset (a handful of recent hurricanes) to keep the download small and the plot understandable.

Source

  • Publisher: NOAA National Hurricane Center (NHC)
  • URL: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/#hurdat
  • Direct file (Atlantic basin, current): https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/hurdat/hurdat2-1851-2023-051124.txt
  • Documentation: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/hurdat/hurdat2-format-atl-1851-2023.pdf

Public domain. As a US federal government dataset, HURDAT2 is in the public domain under 17 U.S.C. § 105. Attribution to NOAA/NHC is expected as a matter of practice but not legally required.

Coordinate reference system

Latitude/longitude in WGS 84 (EPSG:4326), decimal degrees. The file itself stores coordinates as degrees + hemisphere codes (e.g. "27.5N", "80.2W") — the loader in Lab 0 converts to signed decimal degrees.

Attributes and measurement scales

Attribute Type Measurement scale Notes
storm_id text Nominal e.g. "AL092023" for the 9th storm of 2023
name text Nominal Assigned name once storm reaches tropical-storm status; "UNNAMED" for pre-1950 storms and some others
date_time datetime Interval (with an origin) 6-hourly synoptic times (00, 06, 12, 18 UTC), plus occasional off-synoptic entries at landfall
record_id text Nominal Flags special observations (e.g. "L" for landfall)
status categorical Nominal TD (tropical depression), TS (tropical storm), HU (hurricane), EX (extratropical), etc.
latitude / longitude numeric Ratio Signed decimal degrees, WGS 84
max_wind numeric Ratio Maximum 1-minute sustained wind in knots (kt); can be missing (-999) for very early records
min_pressure numeric Ratio Minimum central pressure in millibars (mb); frequently missing before ~1979 satellite era
wind_radii_* numeric Ratio Radii of 34-kt, 50-kt, 64-kt winds in four quadrants (post-2004 only)

Known caveats

  • Data quality is uneven across time. Records before the 1940s rely on shipping observations and are missing or approximate for many storms. Records before the 1979 satellite era are missing many pressure and wind-radii fields.
  • Missing values are coded as -999, not as NaN. Any Python loader must convert these explicitly or descriptive statistics will be garbage.
  • Best-track data is reanalyzed periodically. The file version changes yearly as NOAA reprocesses recent seasons and revises historical estimates. Pin your date range if you want reproducibility.
  • Position accuracy varies. Modern positions are accurate to within tens of kilometers; historical positions may be off by hundreds.
  • 6-hourly points are not tracks. To make a hurricane "line" on a map you have to sort by storm and time, then connect the points — HURDAT2 provides points, not linework.

Why we use it in Week 1

HURDAT2 is a great first spatial dataset: it's public, small enough to load in seconds, visually intuitive (everyone recognizes a hurricane track on a map), and it has all four measurement scales in one file. In Lab 0 we just plot a couple of storm tracks over the Gulf of Mexico to confirm the whole Python + geopandas + matplotlib stack is working end-to-end.

We come back to HURDAT2 in Week 4 (probability distributions — annual hurricane counts) and again in Week 11 (point patterns — landfall locations).
