Week 2 Discussion — When Data Quality Bites

Original post due Friday, September 4, 11:59 pm CT. Two replies due Tuesday, September 8, 11:59 pm CT.

Prompt

This week's reading and video hammered on a single idea: real-world environmental data has problems, and knowing about them before you analyze is the difference between good work and confidently wrong work. Now let's see that idea play out in the wild.

Find one outside source — a peer-reviewed paper, a news article, a data-agency report, a technical blog post — where a data-quality issue affected a conclusion about an environmental question. Some examples of the kinds of stories that qualify:

  • A satellite-based deforestation estimate that turned out to be inflated because clouds were misclassified as clearings.
  • A water-quality assessment that used pre-2000 lab methods and later results, where methodology change created an apparent trend that wasn't real.
  • A biodiversity study using citizen-science data where the "observations" reflected observer effort more than actual species distribution.
  • A climate-normals comparison where a station moved location partway through the record.
  • An EJ analysis where percentile calculations used stale ACS data.

Post one message with:

  1. Title of the source + a link (make sure the link resolves without a paywall if possible; if it's paywalled, tell classmates so they don't waste time).
  2. A ~120-word summary in your own words: what was the environmental question, what data did they use, what was the quality issue, and how did it affect the conclusion?
  3. One sentence naming which of the five data-quality dimensions from lecture (accuracy, precision, completeness, currency, lineage) was most at play. If more than one applies, name the most important one and explain briefly why.

Reply to at least two classmates. Each reply should be at least 80 words and should do one of:

  • Suggest a specific method or check that could have caught the problem earlier in the analysis.
  • Point out a different data-quality dimension you think is also at play in their example, and explain why.
  • Share a related situation you've encountered in your own coursework or field experience.

Avoid "great post!" or one-word reactions.

Why this matters

By the end of this exercise our discussion board will have ~15–25 real examples of data-quality failures in environmental analysis. That's a living reference you can come back to all semester when you're auditing your own data (including your capstone dataset). Take a screenshot or bookmark posts you find especially instructive.

Rubric (5 points)

Points Criteria
5 Substantive original post with link, ~120-word summary in own words, correct assignment of quality dimension; two replies of ≥80 words that advance the discussion.
3 Original post present but thin on summary or wrong on quality-dimension assignment; only one substantive reply.

A note on AI use for this discussion

You may use AI to help you find a source or to help you understand a technical paper. You may not use AI to write your post. The 120-word summary must be in your own voice. Peer reviewers and I can usually tell. If you used AI in your search process, mention it briefly at the end of your post — that's disclosure, not a penalty.
